# Evaluation rubric

Use this rubric as a structured qualitative aid, not as a universal numerical measure of conversation quality.

## Dimensions

- **Information gain:** new facts, evidence, examples, or distinctions.
- **Conceptual gain:** a new explanatory frame, model, or useful reframing.
- **Inferential progress:** a conclusion, contradiction resolved, or reasoning step made explicit.
- **Empirical traction:** a claim becomes testable, falsifiable, or tied to a source.
- **Practical utility:** a decision, plan, artifact, or next action improves.
- **Goal fit:** the exchange serves the user's actual purpose, including emotional or creative goals.

## State definitions

| State | Use when | Do not confuse with |
|---|---|---|
| `ADVANCING` | A meaningful contribution or goal-aligned improvement appears. | Mere length or novelty for novelty's sake. |
| `PRODUCTIVE UNCERTAINTY` | The answer is unsettled, but questions, tensions, or distinctions are getting sharper. | Failure to reach a conclusion immediately. |
| `DIMINISHING RETURNS` | Several substantive turns repeat the same content without new evidence, reasoning, framing, or utility. | Intentional practice, emotional processing, or divergent brainstorming. |
| `READY TO CLOSE` | The user's requested outcome appears sufficiently achieved. | Forcing closure when the user wants to continue. |
| `INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE` | The context or goal is too limited or ambiguous to classify responsibly. | A reason to invent a confident judgment. |

## Warning threshold

Before warning, identify at least two recent substantive turns that add little beyond existing material. Then check for progress that semantic repetition can hide: a sharper definition, a counterexample, a more precise mechanism, an improved artifact, or a user-stated benefit.

A useful warning includes (1) the observed pattern, (2) what has not changed, and (3) one concrete next move. Keep it brief and provisional. If the user chooses to continue freely, respect that choice and avoid nagging.

## Mode-specific safeguards

- **Learning/practice:** repetition is productive when accuracy, fluency, or recall improves.
- **Emotional support:** revisiting a concern may support clarity or regulation; do not demand novelty.
- **Creative exploration:** thematic similarity is acceptable during divergent ideation.
- **Research/argument:** prioritize evidence, mechanisms, counterexamples, and testability.
- **Decision support:** look for new criteria, constraints, evidence, or an explicit decision rule.
